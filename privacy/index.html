Skeasy Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

Skeasy ("the app") routes skiers on the slopes and helps friends find each other. It is made by Ran Abrahami, an individual developer based in Israel, who is responsible for your data (the "controller"). Contact: ranabrahami@gmail.com

The short version

  • Skeasy has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. We don't sell or rent any data.
  • Your location is used on your phone to show where you are and route you.
  • Your location leaves your phone only when you choose: when you share it live with friends, join a group map, send your spot in a message, send a skier report, or turn on "Report lifts I ride".
  • The map is provided by Mapbox, which receives the map tiles your phone asks for and may collect anonymous usage and location data. You can turn that off (see section 4).

1. What stays on your phone

These are kept only on your device. We never receive them. Deleting the app deletes them.

2. Location permission and background location

Skeasy asks for location "While Using the App". When you follow a route or share your location live, Skeasy keeps receiving your location while the phone is locked or in your pocket, and iOS shows the location indicator while it does. It stops as soon as the route ends or sharing stops. You can change or withdraw the permission at any time in iPhone Settings > Skeasy > Location. Without it, the map and routes still work but the app can't show where you are.

3. What leaves your phone, and only when you choose

3.1 Live location sharing and group maps

When you tap Share live, or start or join a group map:

  • Your precise position is sent through a real-time channel run by our provider Supabase (servers in the EU, Ireland) to the friends who have the link you sent. On a group map it also carries the name you typed and the lifts and runs you marked "Closed today", so your friends' routes can avoid them. Updates go out about every 25 metres of movement or every 30 seconds.
  • The channel name is a random code that only travels inside the message you send. Anyone you forward that message to can follow you, so share it only with people you trust.
  • Positions are not stored in a database. They pass through the channel while phones are connected and are gone when you stop.
  • Sharing stops by itself after 3 hours, or when you tap Stop.

3.2 Send my spot

"Send my spot" writes a message with your position in it, which you send yourself with the app you choose (WhatsApp, Messages and so on). The Skeasy link puts the position after a "#", which the browser never sends to our web page. The message also holds an Apple Maps link for friends without Skeasy; if they open it, Apple receives that position under Apple's own privacy policy. The messaging app you pick handles the message under its own policy.

3.3 Skier reports

When you report something (lift closed, queue, icy run, powder, hazard and so on), we store in our Supabase database: the type of report, the resort, which lift or run, the time, your position rounded to about 10 metres, and the random phone ID. Anyone using Skeasy can see the report, its position and time for 6 hours. They never see the phone ID: they get a scrambled value that only tells the app that two reports on the same lift or run came from the same phone. Reports are deleted after about a day. No name or account is attached.

"Report lifts I ride" (off unless you turn it on). The first time Skeasy sees you ride a lift, it asks whether to tell other skiers when a lift is running. If you say yes, then whenever the app is receiving your location (on screen, or while it follows a route or shares your location with the phone locked) and sees you ride a lift most of the way up, it sends a "running" report for that lift. It is stored exactly like a report you tap (the lift, your position rounded to about 10 metres, the time and the random phone ID), at most once per lift every 30 minutes. It only ever says a lift is running. You can turn it on or off at any time in the plan panel.

How reports change routes. When two different phones report a lift or run closed within the hour, Skeasy plans other skiers' routes around it where it can. Reports never close anything outright.

3.4 Sending your trip log

If you tap "Send today's trip log", the file (GPS track and app events for the day) goes wherever you send it. If you send it to us, we use it only to find and fix problems, and delete it when we are done, within 90 days at most.

4. Services the app talks to on its own

Service What it receives Why
Mapbox (map, 3D terrain, offline maps; USA) Your IP address and which map area your phone loads; with telemetry on, anonymous usage and location data (see below) To draw the map
Liftie (liftie.info, lift status) Your IP address and which resort's lift status is asked for To show which lifts are open
Supabase (EU) Your IP address when reading skier reports or sharing live To show skier reports and run live sharing
GitHub Pages (our "meet" link page) Your friend's IP address when they open the link (not the position, see 3.2) To open the link in Skeasy

Like any web server, these services may keep short technical logs (IP address, time, request) for security and operations, under their own policies: Mapbox (Mapbox says it deletes IP addresses within 30 days), Supabase, GitHub. Liftie is a free community project with no published privacy policy; it receives only your IP address and the name of the resort.

Mapbox telemetry. By default the Mapbox map sends Mapbox "de-identified location and usage data", which Mapbox says it promptly anonymizes and aggregates to improve its maps, does not use for advertising, and does not link to your IP address (mapbox.com/telemetry). You can turn this off at any time: tap the "i" on the map and opt out of Mapbox Telemetry.

5. What we don't do

  • No sign-up, no email, no phone number, no contacts, no photos, no payments.
  • No advertising, no third-party analytics or crash-reporting tools, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites.
  • We don't sell, rent or trade personal data.

6. Legal bases (EU and UK users)

  • Live sharing, group maps, Send my spot, skier reports and "Report lifts I ride": your consent (you turn each one on), and providing the service you asked for.
  • Location on your phone: providing the service you asked for, with your permission in iOS.
  • Short server logs and limits on reports (6 per phone per 10 minutes): our legitimate interest in keeping the service safe and working.

How to withdraw consent, at any time: tap Stop on live sharing or leave the group map; turn off "Report lifts I ride" in the plan panel; turn off location in iPhone Settings > Skeasy > Location; opt out of Mapbox Telemetry with the "i" on the map; or delete the app, which erases everything kept on the phone.

8. Where data is processed

Supabase processes data in the EU (Ireland). Mapbox is a US company; its privacy policy describes the safeguards it uses for international transfers. Liftie's server location is not published.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR and similar laws (including Israel's Privacy Protection Law), you can ask to access, correct, delete or restrict your data, object to its use, and complain to your data protection authority. Because Skeasy has no accounts, we usually can't tell which data is yours: almost everything is on your phone (delete the app to erase it), and reports delete themselves within about a day. To exercise a right, email ranabrahami@gmail.com. If you want a report removed sooner, include the resort, the time and the place.

10. Children

Skeasy is not directed at children under 13. Children should use live location sharing only with a parent's agreement. If you think a child has sent us data, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Safety

Skeasy is a guide, not a safety device. Routes come from open map data, skier reports and status sources that can be wrong or out of date. Always follow the resort's signs, closures and ski patrol.

12. Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date above and, for important changes, tell you in the app.
